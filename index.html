Abstract

from the accompanying manuscript

The human kidney contains highly specialized cell populations that coordinate filtration, solute and water homeostasis, blood pressure regulation, endocrine functions, and tissue repair. Although numerous single-cell and single-nucleus studies have profiled the human kidney, differences in cohorts, technologies, analytical pipelines, and annotation frameworks have limited cross-study integration. This fragmentation has limited the ability to define consensus kidney cell states, identify rare disease-associated populations and interpret kidney disease genetics in a cellular context.

Here, we assembled 18 human kidney single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq datasets spanning 232 donors and five major disease contexts into a uniformly processed and computationally integrated Human Kidney Cell Atlas (HKCA). This effort comprises over one million high-quality cells (816,895) / nuclei (215,308) and resolves 120 harmonized cell states across epithelial, endothelial, stromal, mural, immune, and neural-associated compartments. The HKCA preserved major kidney lineages while resolving rare and specialized populations, including discrete renin-producing mural cell populations, erythropoietin-associated Norn cells, NEGR1+ immune-attracting fibroblasts, extracellular-matrix–modulating podocytes, and a CCL2+ ascending thin limb state linked to kidney-function genetic risk. Integration with spatial transcriptomics, ligand–receptor analysis, and genetic association data further defined the microenvironmental context and disease relevance of these populations and nominated candidate pathways for therapeutic investigation.

Beyond atlas construction, the HKCA provides a generalizable framework for automated annotation of independent kidney datasets across sample types, modalities and species, including urine-derived cells and spatial transcriptomics samples. Cross-species projection between human and mouse kidney atlases further identified conserved and divergent cellular programs, providing guidance for interpreting experimental mouse models of kidney disease. Together, the HKCA establishes an informed reference for human kidney biology, enabling the discovery of rare cell states, interpretation of disease-associated remodeling, and cellular localization of genetic risk.
